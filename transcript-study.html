1. How the machine works

Spending is someone else's income. Credit spends tomorrow's income today. Interest is the price of that time.

The film opens on a cup of coffee. The price becomes the next person's pay, and a part of it comes back as wages. When the price of money rises, borrowing and spending slow.

2. The eight storms, in the film's order

  1. 0:00. Nearly three billion dollars a day goes to interest. Not to roads. Not to schools.
  2. 2:46. Hormuz. Oil through the strait. The film says traffic there fell by about seventy percent, and gasoline later jumped by more than nine percent.
  3. 5:50. The price tag. The film says consumer prices in September were up 3.7 percent on the year. Core inflation about 2.6 percent.
  4. 8:25. The hike. A boxed-in Federal Reserve. The film says the September range was 3.75 to 4 percent.
  5. 11:15. The bond market. The film says the ten-year yield went over 5 percent, and the thirty-year near 5.5 percent.
  6. 13:49. The whirlpool. Old debt rolled into a higher price of money. The film says net interest was a record 1.5 trillion dollars in the fiscal year, and the deficit near 2 trillion.
  7. 17:33. The AI boom. The film says the largest AI and chip names lost about a trillion dollars of market value in a week.
  8. 21:50. Europe. France, Japan, and a debt that can travel.
  9. 24:31. The sinkhole. Private credit. The film says more than one student-loan dollar in ten was ninety days late or worse.

3. The cases that touch a person

  1. The house. A four hundred thousand dollar loan. The film says a thirty-year mortgage near 7.3 percent costs about 2,750 dollars a month, against about 1,700 dollars at 3 percent. About a thousand dollars more a month.
  2. The car. The film says car loans follow the same ten-year yield.
  3. The card. A rate that can rise after the money is already owed.
  4. Cash. The film says keep some cash you can reach.

4. The fair-weather check, about 26:50

The film says the weather has not broken yet. Unemployment about 4.2 percent. Announced layoffs in September lower than the year before. The banks that reported looked sound. A small bounce in housing. The next debt-ceiling fight dated 2027. A court sent more than 120 billion dollars in tariff refunds back to importers.

5. What it means for you, about 30:21

Know the rate on each loan. Keep cash you can reach. Do not buy a house or a car in a panic. Look at the month, not only the advertised price.

The film names later dates to watch: a Federal Reserve decision, an election, a trade truce, and the next rate forecast. Those dates belong to the film. This desk does not move them.
