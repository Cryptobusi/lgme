Fundamental standing

In a court, standing is the threshold. A party needs a concrete stake in the outcome. The injury has to be traceable, and the court has to be able to address it. Without that, the claim is not one the court will hear. A court does not invent the case. It recognizes a case from the facts and from the rules already in force.

On this desk, standing is attested provenance. A claim to Campus Credit, or a clearing obligation, exists when a delivery has been confirmed. Members mint credit on verified delivery. The pair is a credit and an equal clearing mark. The Third Seat does not create that pair, and the Third Seat does not mint.

Members mint credit on verified delivery; the Third Seat — the $lgme self-regulating nonprofit — audits and is the arbiter who can veto; it holds $lgme only in trust for the Floor's posterity and takes no seigniorage.

That is the judicial posture in this analogy: the boundary can decide because the parties put an authenticated record in front of it, not because the boundary owns what they exchanged. Creo works alongside the US dollar. A dollar obligation outside the ledger stays a dollar obligation. State sovereignty is unchanged.

Precedent as a shore-up

Common-law systems steady expectations with prior decisions. A later case with the same material facts is guided by the earlier one. The body of decisions is public. Someone who wants a different result has to say why. The history makes a quiet rewrite expensive.

Creo's public tape is the same kind of shore-up. A fulfillment, a clearing, a refusal, and a veto stay visible. Later lines are read against that record and against rules stated in advance: Floor claims rank first inside lgme, credit is minted on verified delivery, and unpaid debts sunset on a defined term while surpluses never expire.

The tape does not command the way a statute commands. It supplies the authenticated history the rules are applied to. A closed circle can net to zero in Campus Credit. The November draft illustrated that with three neighbors. Those amounts are SAMPLE. They are not a recorded exchange. The clearing engine draws a sample loop that sums to zero. It does not call the network.

When this note says adjunction or colimit, those words are modelling metaphors. They name the picture of a delivery audited back onto a tape, and of a closed circle netting out. They are not a proof, and they are not a function this site runs.

Supporting logic

Both pictures rest on one conservation point.

  • A court does not create property out of nothing. It recognizes relations that are already in the facts and in the governing rules.
  • The Third Seat does not mint credit in advance and does not take seigniorage. It certifies that members minted a zero-sum pair on verified delivery, and that a closed circle may net out. It is the arbiter who can veto.

Floor Seniority is the priority rule inside lgme: Floor claims rank first, the way some claims rank ahead of ordinary ones in a bankruptcy schedule. Ranking Floor claims ahead of general commercial debt, outside this ledger, would require legislation: laws, mandates, and tax incentives. This site does not claim that outside creditors are already bound.

The debt sunset is the limitation rule. Unpaid debts sunset on a defined term. The loss is absorbed by the subfloor and the sponsors. Surpluses never expire. After 90 days the member covenant gives a gentle reminder and the option to gift surplus to the Floor. The length of the debt term is not a live setting. The draft's "two generations" figure is SAMPLE / unverified.

The veto is the check. It stops a line that was not verified. It does not spend, and it does not replace a court. Creo works alongside the US dollar. The three parties stay the State (US$), the Floor (Campus Credit), and the Third Seat ($lgme).

Limits of the parallel

The analogy is about institutional logic: an authenticated history, and a boundary that can say no without becoming a party to the trade. Creo is not positive law. A mathematical picture on another page does not prove a theorem about primes. The Riemann Hypothesis remains unproven, and it is not the subject of this page.

Extending Floor Seniority beyond the internal ledger to general commercial debt would require legislation, the way a new priority in commercial law would. The strength of the design is that the rules are stated in advance, applied to a public record, and constrained by a boundary that owns nothing it adjudicates and holds $lgme only in trust.

In short: the Third Seat is the arbiter who can veto. Its authority, in this analogy, comes from authenticated facts plus a visible history of earlier resolutions. The parallel is precise there. It stops where a lawyer would need a statute, a filing, or a judgment.
