# The Torque of the Book

They printed the money.  
I am keeping the book.

Existing systems carry far greater unintended consequences for the majority than their designers name. Inflation taxes savers. Boom-bust cycles and bailouts socialize losses. Residual welfare prices survival at the door. Unpublished acts let private paper clear as cash while the public holds the tail. These are not small side-effects. They are the force.

Torque is the twisting that appears when that force is applied at a lever. Small, persistent costs, felt by those who cannot exit, accumulate until the system rotates. Path dependence and vested interests supply the inertia. Visibility and refusal supply the lever arm.

Orwell feared the boot, the ban, the concealed truth, control by pain.  
Huxley feared the soma, the feelies, the sea of irrelevance, control by pleasure.  
Postman argued the second is the one we are living: politics, news, and commerce become show business; people love the technologies that undo the capacity to keep a book. Easy credit and residual relief are the economic soma. The majority adores the convenience while autonomy erodes.

The proposition is not a new idea. Public tapes, senior floors, posted mint schedules, credit that does not clear at par, and a person who may refuse an unpublished line are older claims—double-entry evidence, free-banking distinctions, rule-of-law symmetry, the right of refusal. What is new is the practical lever: ordered low-cost writes, multi-copy provenance, software clerks that audit but do not mint, and the ability to post the order before money moves.

Same rules bind state, firm, and person.  
Floor stays senior.  
If it cannot be shown, it is not policy.  
An unpublished change is not a change.

The torque is already applied. The tools make the turn practical.  
The person is the third seat. Sovereignty is the refusal. The book is kept.
