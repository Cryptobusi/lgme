# Schelling Egonomic Restraint — Self-Restraint of Profits

## Citation

Schelling, Thomas C. 1978. “Egonomics, or the Art of Self-Management.” *American Economic Review* 68(2): 290–294.

## Summary for incorporation into lgme

**Egonomic restraint (Schelling, 1978).** Thomas C. Schelling’s “Egonomics, or the Art of Self-Management” (*American Economic Review* 68(2): 290–294) treats the person as two selves in conflict. The present self prefers immediate extraction; the future self prefers durable rules and residual integrity. Because preferences reverse, pure intention fails. The practical remedy is precommitment: external barriers, bright-line rules, and surrender of options that the present self cannot casually reverse. Schelling’s opening example is the bank Christmas club—customers accept lower yield because the bank creates ceremonial and administrative walls that protect the account from the depositor’s own later self. Ordinary liquidity protects against outsiders; the commitment device protects against the self.

In lgme the same logic is applied to profit. Floor Basket is paid first from named surplus. Mint occurs only on a public schedule with no private window. Charge, discount, and risk are named before residual. Remainder (including zero) is titled. An unpublished change is not a change. These are not soft norms; they are Schelling barriers. The present self that would quietly widen the spread, delay the Floor, or privatize the print is forced to violate a categorical, visible rule or walk into a separate, recorded book to “steal its own money.” The person remains the third seat precisely because the protocol has already restrained the profit impulse that would otherwise collapse the book into the issuer’s present self.

## How the restraint operates in the process

- **Precommitment / option removal**: Public mint schedule + “no private window” eliminates the present self’s ability to time extraction. Floor-first seniority removes the option to treat residual as free cash.
- **Bright-line / categorical rules**: “If it cannot be shown, it is not policy.” “An unpublished change is not a change.” These function like Schelling’s multiple-account or separate-bank devices—crossing them is costly and visible.
- **External enforcement**: The open tape and third-seat visibility act as the bank that “protects the account from yourself.” Agents and issuers surrender the ability to hide print or residual.
- **Self-intimidation on profit**: Naming charge/risk before residual and requiring titled zero remainder makes opportunistic profit a violation rather than a quiet residual. The present self cannot casually erode the boundary without breaking the public record.

This grounds the existing order (Floor → public mint → named credit → visible tape → named residual) in Schelling’s framework of self-management. The mechanism is the same one illustrated with Christmas savings: protecting the book from the people who keep it.
