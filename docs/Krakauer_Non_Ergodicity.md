# Krakauer on Non-Ergodicity and the Book

David Krakauer (Santa Fe Institute) argues that real economies are non-ergodic complex adaptive systems. Temporary shocks are not washed away. History locks in. Individual trajectories permanently diverge from population averages.

## The examination

Mainstream models often assume ergodicity: the long-run average of the system matches the long-run experience of any individual. Krakauer says this fails.

- Path dependence and lock-in: early contingencies create self-reinforcing loops. Once a path is chosen, return to an alternate state becomes impossible even if it was better.
- Broken symmetries and irreversibility: recessions, technological shifts, and institutional rules do not deviate from a fixed baseline; they change the baseline.
- Time averages versus space averages: the average wealth of a thousand people at one moment does not predict what a typical person experiences over a thousand days. Positive feedback makes a few paths compound while others decay.

Economies therefore evolve more like biological histories than like textbook equilibrium physics. Complexity economics, not finer equilibrium models, is the relevant frame.

## How this reinforces the book

Non-ergodicity removes the warrant for treating visibility, a senior floor, and an individual right of refusal as optional.

- The force is path-dependent cost. Small unpublished acts and residual floors accumulate on those who cannot exit.
- Visibility and refusal are the lever. A public tape and the rule “if it cannot be shown, it is not policy” make the chosen path readable while it is still contestable.
- The third seat addresses the divergence of individual trajectories from the average. Same rules bind state, firm, and person. A senior floor paid from named surplus does not restore ergodicity; it gives the individual a claim that does not depend on the average eventually catching up.
- Precommitment (posted mint, credit that does not clear at par, an amendment path that cannot be shortened in the same act) is the response to irreversibility. Once the feasible set has shifted, the only remaining protection is a rule that was already visible and senior.
