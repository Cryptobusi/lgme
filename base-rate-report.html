Base rate in realistic terms

Places the mock’s ~28–36% (median ~32%) recorded-value gain against published public figures. Design research. Not an offer. Mock runs report · Options · Desk

What the mock base rate is

Across 24 seeded runs the productivity gain sits between about 28% and 36%. The median is near 32%. It is the share of existing acts (tutoring, neighbor help, light care, study groups) that become visible on the tape because the cost of recording them falls to a consensus timestamp. It is not new money and not a mint. Coins issued stay conserved.

How the mock 32% sits against those figures

The mock is smaller than the unpaid-care total, and that is correct. ILO and OECD put unpaid care and household services at roughly 9–15% of GDP (replacement) and higher on opportunity cost. The mock does not claim to capture all of that. It captures only the subset of acts that would clear under the protocol rules (floor first, named credit, public tape, charge separate) inside the three cases.

The rail cost explains why the subset vanishes today. A published flat rate of 2.9% + $0.30 makes a $5 or $10 act uneconomic to record. On a $5 ticket the effective rate is about 9%. The protocol’s claim is that a consensus timestamp removes that barrier for acts that already happen. The 32% is the modeled share of those acts that then appear on the tape.

Conservation is the difference from a GDP add-on. Satellite accounts add unpaid work to GDP. The mock does not expand the unit. It records existing work against a conserved coin supply. The gain is visibility and usable residual after the floor, not an increase in the money stock.

Realistic reading for a deployment

A school, nursing home, or neighborhood already performing these acts would not “create” 32% more money. It would see a portion of work that currently leaves no balance-sheet trace become named and residual-eligible after the floor. The published unpaid-care shares (9–15%+ of GDP) show the pool is large. The rail fee shows why the small end of that pool never clears. The mock shows the shape under the stated invariants. A live system would still have to keep floor first, public tape, named charge, and third-seat refusal.
