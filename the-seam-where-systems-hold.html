Three neighbors, no cash needed

In the supplied plain-English turn, Rafi, Ana, and Marcus each do $100 of work for one another over a weekend: plumbing, meals, and a porch repair. Looked at one IOU at a time, $300 of debt hangs over the block, and a bank would charge interest while they wait for dollars.

If the IOUs form a closed circle, the shared loop can be merged and wiped: each neighbor is owed $100 and owes $100, so the net is zero. The work still happened, and a record of that work can remain, but nobody needs a federal dollar to settle the circle.

If the circle is uneven—Rafi and Ana each deliver $100, Marcus $60—the shared $60 still cancels ($180 of gross debt erased). Ana is even. Only the leftover $40 stays on the books as a named remaining obligation, not as a forever debt.

That automatic wiping is a design picture, not something this site runs today.

Floor first, and no forever debts

Floor Seniority is the groceries-before-luxury rule: a member’s baseline for food, shelter, utilities, care, and basic dignity stays first in line, ahead of commercial or enterprise balances. A rough winter must not lock someone out of the essentials.

The two-generation debt sunset is the no-forever-debts rule: unpaid balances, and hoarded surplus, are not meant to last indefinitely or pass to a child. That window is a design aim. Clearing windows stay on hold until there is a deployment and enough data.
