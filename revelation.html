Revelation

What the Egonomic Sovereign Provenance Protocol would have changed in the system that existed before the Federal Reserve. A reading. Not a claim that the protocol was then in force.

Not a mint. Not an offer. Not a history of an office that existed.

The constraint on expansion

Expansions would still have been constrained by the requirement that expansions appear on a public schedule and respect floor seniority. AI could have helped monitor real-time coverage of the floor and flag deviations, reducing the scope for discretionary overshoot.

What it would not have removed

The protocol would not have eliminated solvency problems arising from bad loans, real-estate bubbles, or genuine resource shortages. It also assumes sufficient participation in the tape. In a severe crisis some actors might still attempt parallel opaque claims. Enforcement of refusal ultimately relies on counterparties honoring the record or on legal recognition of the tape — neither of which is automatic.

Net assessment

With contemporary AI and DLT, the Egonomic SPP would have been a stronger stabilizer of the pre-Fed system than the combination of bond-backed notes, private clearing houses, and ad-hoc Treasury deposits that actually existed. It would have expanded usable liquidity under scarcity constraints, subjected state claims to the same visible order as private ones, and forced more explicit settlements. That path would have made the later institutional choice of a large, independent central bank with an expandable balance sheet less necessary and would have left fewer residual problems of the kind now visible in the Fed’s securities portfolio, interest payments, and exposure to fiscal pressure.

It remains an overlay on existing money rather than a full replacement of base-money management. Real scarcity and political control of the ultimate unit of account would still have required resolution, only under tighter public rules.

Floor stays senior. Same rules bind state, firm, and person. If it cannot be shown, it is not policy.
