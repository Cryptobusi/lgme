Three parties

Clearing here has three parties, and they work alongside one another. Creo is Campus Credit for a confirmed deed. It sits beside the US dollar and beside state sovereignty. It does not stand in for either.

Three parties. Walls between the units stay up. Nothing on this page converts one unit into another.

A sample clearing circle

The November 2026 draft told this with three neighbors and dollar amounts. Those amounts are SAMPLE / unverified. This page keeps the shape and drops the amounts.

Neighbors A, B, and C each deliver one sample unit of Floor service around a closed circle. Looked at one promise at a time, three promises are open. Netted inside Campus Credit, each person is owed one unit and owes one unit, so the circle is zero. The work stays on the record: the provenance of each delivery remains. A dollar price, if one was agreed outside the circle, remains a State US$ matter. The Third Seat audits the three deliveries and can veto a line that was not verified. Members minted the credit. The Third Seat did not.

If the circle is uneven, only the shared part nets to zero. The leftover is a named remainder in Campus Credit, still subject to Floor Seniority and to the debt sunset below. The clearing engine draws a sample three-way loop that sums to zero. It is a canvas with sample data. It does not settle a live circle, and it does not call the network.

Floor Seniority

Inside lgme, Floor claims rank first. A Floor service is ahead of other claims on this ledger.

Ranking Floor claims ahead of general commercial debt, outside this ledger, would require legislation: laws, mandates, and tax incentives. This site does not claim that outside creditors are already bound.

The Floor's defaults, as drawn on the workflow map, are transparency, UBI, and AI faculties. On that map the UBI entry is a UBI-like Floor earned by confirmed deeds, not a cash transfer this site pays, and the AI entry is an assistive faculty a person can refuse.

Debt sunset

Unpaid debts sunset on a defined term. The loss is absorbed by the subfloor and the sponsors. The length of that term is not a live setting. The November 2026 draft called the window two generations. That length is SAMPLE / unverified.

Surpluses never expire. After 90 days the member covenant gives a gentle reminder, with the option to gift surplus to the Floor. There is no automatic penalty and no seizure. That 90-day reminder is the clarification already written beside the member covenant. It is not a deletion of a positive balance.

The subfloor is a local reserve with published caps. It is not a bank balance, and this page does not start an automatic write-off.

Creo and time parity

Creo works alongside the US dollar and state sovereignty. A member mints Campus Credit when the person who received the help confirms the delivery. One verified deed is the unit. Spending it cancels it. It is not $lgme, and it is not cash.

1-for-1 time parity applies inside Floor services: an hour of Floor service clears against an hour of Floor service. Enterprise goods stay priced. A priced good does not become an hour by being written on this ledger.

Campus deeds are on Campus. The mutual-credit screen can rehearse a line. Neither screen is changed by this page.

The Third Seat

Members mint credit on verified delivery; the Third Seat — the $lgme self-regulating nonprofit — audits and is the arbiter who can veto; it holds $lgme only in trust for the Floor's posterity and takes no seigniorage.

The Third Seat does not mint. A veto stops a line. It does not spend, and it does not replace a court. The longer curriculum, including the person's own refusal, stays on the Third Seat.

A category-theory sketch in the November draft pictured verified delivery and the audit as an adjunction, with a closed circle as a colimit. That sketch is an analogy for the sentence above. It is not a proof, and it is not a function this site runs.

Known risks and safeguards

Three failures are already visible in a mutual-credit circle:

  • A member leaves while negative. The open debit has no counter-service behind it.
  • Free-riding. Someone draws Floor services and does not return work.
  • Collusive fake loops. A closed circle of confirmations with no delivery.

Safeguards:

  • Covenant -50/-200 limits. The member covenant caps a negative balance at -50 Creo, and at -200 only after community review. At the cap, outbound debits suspend until counter-services restore the balance. The limits are in Creo. A workout does not mint Creo.
  • Two sponsors, at least one outside a closed circle. The covenant records two sponsor signatures. The gate asks that one sponsor be from the member's group, so a circle cannot confirm itself alone.
  • Separate-device and passkey confirmation. See One-Tap Entry. A second confirm belongs on the other person's device.
  • A public tape. The record of the line stays visible. A refusal stays on it.
  • Third Seat veto. The $lgme self-regulating nonprofit audits and is the arbiter who can veto.

Speculative background: ΣRH

Speculative analogy · not a proof

The Riemann Hypothesis remains unproven. The November 2026 report sketched a four-axiom picture, called ΣRH here, in the style of a Hilbert–Pólya postulate: a self-adjoint boundary whose real eigenvalues would place the non-trivial zeros on the critical line. That axiom assumes the hypothesis. It does not prove it. Showing that such a system can exist without contradiction would still be a proof, and this page does not claim one.

The analogy, and only the analogy, is that a two-sided system needs a boundary that neither side writes alone. On this site that boundary is the Third Seat sentence in the section above, not a theorem about primes.
