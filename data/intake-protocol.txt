Member Intake: Productive Capacity Vetting Protocol
This instrument audits and validates the two declared offerings required under Section 2 of the Member Covenant. Its purpose is to ensure that all credit issued across the circle represents unencumbered, physically verifiable human capacity rather than speculative promises or debt-leveraged intermediation.

Part II: The 5-Point Vetting Assessment Framework
Sponsoring members and admissions stewards evaluate each declared offering against five pass/fail gates. Both offerings must achieve a PASS on all five criteria before the initial negative clearing threshold is granted.
| Gate | Assessment Criterion | Passing Standard | Red Flags (Auto-Fail) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Grounded Reality (Physicality) | The offering consists of real, demonstrable skills or physical utility directly usable by household or community members. | Essential maintenance, food production, childcare, fabrication, physical hauling, direct professional/technical labor. | Abstract consulting, multi-level marketing, dropshipping, speculative coaching, or vague "ideation." |
| 2. Unencumbered Tooling (Zero-Debt) | The applicant owns and controls the tools, workspace, or seed materials necessary to fulfill the offering. | Hand tools, verified software environment, owned workshop/kitchen, existing physical inventory. | Equipment rented on credit, dependence on proprietary paywalled APIs, or requiring circle funds to buy initial tools. |
| 3. Non-Intermediated Labor (Directness) | The offering represents the applicant’s own direct exertion and time, not brokered or subcontracted work. | Performed personally by the applicant; transparent execution. | Reselling goods made by third parties, outsourcing labor to non-members, or automated scraping/arbitrage. |
| 4. Discrete Quantifiability (Clearing Metric) | The offering can be measured in clean, repeatable units tied directly to time and effort. | Standardized units: "1 cord of stacked firewood," "2 hours of carpentry," "1 complete mechanical inspection." | Open-ended retainers, subjective "value pricing," or unbounded commitments that cannot be audited. |
| 5. Realistic Capacity (Solvency) | The declared monthly volume reflects true availability alongside the applicant’s normal life demands. | 4–15 hours/month of dedicated circle capacity; sustainable rhythm. | Over-promising 40+ hours/week, or under-promising less than 1 unit/month (inability to clear debits). |
