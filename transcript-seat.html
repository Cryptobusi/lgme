The third seat, told simply

The narration the film was made to speak, in order. Each part is about fifteen seconds. Plain words. Not a new coin, and not financial advice.

0:00 The simple part

Here is the simple part. When you buy something, that money becomes someone else's pay. That is fair. The trouble is a secret extra promise. That is not a math mistake. It is a me-first habit that nobody stopped.

0:15 Three chairs

Picture three chairs. One is for the public rules. One is for the shop. The third chair is yours. You do not boss the other two. You check them. Same rules. Same book. If you cannot see it, it is not a real rule.

0:30 Four tools

Your chair has four tools, and they are friendly. A notebook everyone can read. Needs that were named get paid first. You vote by what you do, not by a hidden stamp. And nobody gets to print in secret.

0:45 You may say no

You may say no, and that is a good power. If a line skipped the line, or nobody can show it, you refuse it. Your no stays written, and you say why. A blank no does not count. One honest no keeps the book fair.

1:00 The desk

The desk is a table for real things. You set down what you have. You name what you want. A book. A Tuesday. A lesson. Help. Both sides have to be there. You see the price before you say yes. A friend and a stranger get the same price.

1:15 Coffee

The film starts with a cup of coffee. The money becomes the next person's pay. IdidIT writes that trade before anyone says yes. Both people can point to it. The circle stays fair.

1:30 Gasoline

The film says gasoline jumped by more than nine percent. This desk cannot change the price at the pump. It can trade a ride, a tool, or help, and the price is written first.

1:45 The house and the car

The film's house is a four hundred thousand dollar loan. The month is about two thousand seven hundred fifty dollars, not one thousand seven hundred. The car loan rides the same high price. The desk does not cut those bills. Look at the month. You may say no.

2:00 The card, the school loan, the cash

A card can cost more after you already owe. In the film, more than one school-loan dollar in ten is badly late. The desk does not erase that. A pause is not a payment. A late promise is not cash. Keep the book at home, next to cash you can reach.

2:15 A free share of the pie

The pie gets bigger when you add something real. A tool. A Tuesday. A lesson. Or help. The Sovereign Provenance Protocol lets you put that in for free. No ticket. No secret fee. Your part counts because it is written.

2:30 The same short rules

The new rules are short, so the door stays light. Needs named first. The price is shown. Same price for everyone. You may say no. The public, the shop, you, and the helper all keep those rules.

2:45 The helper and the ledger

Two tools keep the share fair. Your own helper can explain. It cannot post, hold, or settle. A public ledger keeps the tape where people can read it, so a line cannot hide. That is how more of the pie can be shared.

3:00 The close

You are not too small for this. You are the checker. The book stays with you, and you can carry it home. A company, a helper, and a machine all follow the same rule. Fair is something you can point to. That is the third seat.
