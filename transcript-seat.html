0:00 The circuit

A purchase is someone else's income. That circuit is ordinary. The failure is not the arithmetic. It is a claim the other parties cannot see. Economists call that information asymmetry.

0:15 Three seats

Three seats share one table. The public may tax and schedule a mint. The firm may issue. The person may refuse any line that skipped the order or cannot be shown. The third seat audits. It does not manage.

0:30 The order of claims

The order is the one a balance sheet already uses. Named needs are senior, and they are met first. A residual comes after, and it may be zero. A pause is dated forbearance, not a payment. A receivable is not cashed at par.

0:45 The surplus

A person can add to the surplus at low cost. A tool, an hour, a lesson, or care goes on the shelf. No ticket. No hidden spread. The public, the firm, the person, and the assistant keep the same short rules. The assistant may explain. It cannot sign.

1:00 The principal

A shared ledger makes a quiet print expensive, because the tape can be read. More of the surplus can be shared, because the share can be pointed to. A system that no longer answers to a principal is not a better tool. It is a replacement. The protocol keeps the principal in the chair.
