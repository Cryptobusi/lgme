Eleven tries to replace gold: why money fails

Source: Gabriel Mamou-Mani on LinkedIn. His headline was “Clarifying the Crypto Story.” This is his account, in short lines. The post says eleven tries. Nine are listed here.

  • 1000s, China: he says paper notes were printed to pay for wars and were worthless within a century.
  • 1716, France: John Law backed paper with company shares. The bubble broke, then people rioted.
  • 1775, America: the Continental dollar lost 99% in five years. The saying was “not worth a Continental.”
  • 1789, France: assignats were backed by confiscated land and were wiped out by 1796.
  • 1862, America: greenbacks brought inflation, then a 17-year crawl back to gold.
  • 1923, Germany: the Rentenmark was backed by mortgages. It worked at home. Foreign merchants refused it.
  • 1944: Keynes proposed the bancor, a world currency. The United States said no.
  • 1969: the IMF created the SDR. He calls it a formula, not money people use.
  • 1971: the dollar left gold. He notes central banks buying 1,000 or more tonnes of gold a year, three years running.

His thesis

He says every attempt died from one of two causes: “The issuer printed too much. Or the people next door would not take it.” Bitcoin, on his telling, removes the first cause by code. The second is still open.

Where lgme.app fits

Cause 1 is overprinting. A deed credit is created only against a committed deed, and it settles when both people confirm an IdidIt (verified receipt). The public tape makes Quiet Print, hidden issuance, visible. The subfloor will not cover a discount past half the pool, past 20 $lgme a day for one person, or at all when the pool is empty. It does not borrow from the floor.

Cause 2 is the neighbor who will not take it. That is the open problem here. The answers on the desk start with acceptance: a campus time-and-deed pilot, merchant redemption, category subfloors, and a fallback to barter or pay-in-terms. The Rentenmark and the bancor are the caution. One worked at home and was refused abroad. The other was a careful design that nobody adopted.

Open questions

  • What would make a merchant outside the pilot accept a deed credit?
  • Does the $lgme US$ peg help acceptance, or does it get in the way?
  • If the credit works inside one campus, what is the first step that lets the next town take it?

Crypto neobank / card stack: the infrastructure layers

Gleb Krivosheev described the infrastructure layers behind a crypto neobank and a crypto card stack. Source: Gleb Krivosheev on LinkedIn

The post says there are eight layers. Seven were relayed. The eighth is not listed here. Names are plain text. This list is not a partnership, endorsement, or integration.

Card issuing

Rain, Wirex, Immersve, Monavate, Reap.

This layer mints the card a person can tap or type at a shop.

Where lgme.app fits. It does not issue cards.

Card Processing

Lithic, Thredd, Paymentology, Marqeta, i2c Inc., SoFi Tech Solutions, Highnote, Episode Six.

This layer carries a card payment from the shop to the issuer.

Where lgme.app fits. It does not process cards.

Pay-ins and payouts

BVNK, Conduit, Lightspark, Bridge, Zero Hash, Transak, Yellow Card, MoonPay.

This layer moves money in and out, between fiat and crypto.

Where lgme.app fits. The desk is non-custodial. It holds no keys or seeds and sends no transactions. A member’s own wallet signs a real $lgme payment.

Fiat accounts and rails

OpenPayd, Banking Circle, Clear Junction, Nium.

This layer holds ordinary-money accounts and the bank rails under them.

Where lgme.app fits. It holds no fiat account. Floor and subfloor pools are local reserves on the tape. They are a picture of a reserve, not a bank balance.

Balances and ledger

Formance, Modern Treasury. Onchain balances on chains like Solana and Plasma.

This layer is the book that says who has what.

Where lgme.app fits. Its book is the tamper-evident tape: an IdidIt (verified receipt) and the daily Merkle root. It does not use Formance or Modern Treasury, and it does not post those balances on a chain. Public Hedera anchoring is coming. It is not written to Hedera today.

Wallets and custody

Fireblocks, DFNS, BitGo, Privy.

This layer stores keys for someone else, or builds the wallet those keys live in.

Where lgme.app fits. It is non-custodial. It holds no keys or seeds and sends no transactions.

Risk and compliance

Sumsub, Sardine, Persona, Veriff, iDenfy, TRM Labs.

This layer checks identity and watches for abuse.

Where lgme.app fits. The exchange flow already has a local KYC step and a 6-digit code. That is the check on the desk today. These firms are not wired in.

Open questions

  • Which on-ramp or off-ramp would fit a deed-credit barter system that keeps no custody?
  • Is a card layer ever needed, or is merchant redemption inside the group enough?
  • Should the tape stay the only ledger, with an outside rail used only when a member chooses to settle in $lgme?

Toward Deployable Hardware Roots of Trust for Edge AI: Software PUFs and Their Trade-offs

Yilin Li and Farhad Merchant, University of Groningen.

Abstract · PDF

Plain English

The paper surveys 37 software PUF methods. Each one derives a unique, repeatable device fingerprint from chips that are already there: memory start-up values, DRAM timing and decay, flash, processor timing, or motion sensors. It uses only software, so a secret key never has to be stored on the device. The survey compares how unique and reliable each method is, how fast it answers (microseconds to minutes), and whether it works while the device is running. Four problems stay open. Low-level hardware access is often locked by firmware. Enough randomness has to remain after error correction. Some methods are too slow to use while a program is running. The attacker model is not always clear: someone who controls the operating system could replay or tamper with the answer.

Why it matters for lgme.app

lgme already binds trust to the device: passkey and fingerprint unlock are planned, the tape stays on the device, and Sources can use on-device AI. A fingerprint like this could later strengthen a “one device, one member” check and protect on-device keys and models without new hardware. It needs kernel or firmware access that a web app does not have. For now, WebAuthn passkeys, which use the phone’s secure chip, remain the practical path. Software PUFs are a watch item, not a build item.

Open questions

  • Could a phone vendor or a wallet expose a PUF-backed key through WebAuthn?
  • Can it help stop duplicate accounts in the campus pilot without collecting personal data?
