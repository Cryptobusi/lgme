1. The 5,000-Year Trade and the 2002 Rupture

In Plato’s Phaedrus, King Thamus warned that writing is not an elixir for remembering, but for reminding—substituting internal generative capability for external storage. Yet civilization made this trade for five millennia because external media had an unnamed feature: it could walk out of a burning building in human hands. Clay tablets, vellum codices, and paper survive across centuries with zero watts of continuous power.

In 2002, humanity crossed an unprecedented historical threshold: over 50% of all stored information became digital (today exceeding 97%). In doing so, we traded physical, durable continuity for an ephemeral infrastructure that decays exponentially and demands continuous electricity, active subscriptions, and monopolistic hosting.
