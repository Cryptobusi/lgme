Barter, exchange, and named credit

Simple summary. IdidIt-style credit creation on lgme.app sits closer to modern organized barter (trade credits inside a network) than to a general monetary exchange. It loosens the double coincidence of wants inside the network without becoming general money. Effects on the wider economy stay limited. Complementary currencies have a long history of exactly this pattern; WIR Bank is the durable Swiss example of a closed-circuit, bank-issued credit that clears among members.

Barter versus exchange

Barter is the exchange of goods or services of roughly equal worth without fiat money. Modern barter exchanges use an internal unit (trade credits) governed by a third party. Exchange is the broader term that includes both barter and currency systems. The primary difference is that ordinary exchange allows money; pure barter does not. Barter is itself a form of exchange.

IdidIt credit creation

lgme.app records acts (goods, time, skills, civic work) on a public tape and can generate named credit that is explicitly not cash, not a deposit, and not a token offered for sale. Settlement options treat tokens as a named promise, not cash at the register. Floor-first rules, public schedule, and visible tape govern issuance. This matches the structure of a modern barter exchange with trade credits more closely than a monetary system in which any seller accepts the unit from any buyer.

Double coincidence of wants

Pure one-to-one barter requires that each party simultaneously wants what the other offers. Organized barter networks and named-credit systems relax this by letting a participant earn credit for one thing and later claim something else inside the network. Matching and valuation frictions remain; the credit is not general purchasing power.

Implications for trade

Asynchronous multi-party matching becomes possible. Previously unrecorded small acts can become visible. Cash can be conserved. Volume stays constrained by network scope, liquidity, and acceptance. Trade favors reciprocal, community-scale, or mutual-aid exchanges and disfavors broad impersonal commerce.

Effect on the rest of the economy

Limited and mostly local. The credits do not expand the broad money supply, do not alter the general price level, and do not interfere with monetary-policy transmission. They can mobilize some idle local resources and conserve fiat for participants, but they remain a parallel restricted circuit.

Complementary currency history

Parallel units have recurred whenever official money was scarce or poorly suited to local needs. Examples include Josiah Warren’s Time Store (1827), Owen’s Labour Exchange (1832), Depression-era scrip, Wörgl (1932), WIR Bank (1934–), Japanese and U.S. time banks (from 1973/1980s), LETS (1983), Ithaca Hours (1991), and Argentine trueque networks. Common pattern: restricted circulation, crisis or niche activation, limited scale, complement rather than substitute for national money.

WIR Bank mutual credit mechanics

Founded 1934 in Switzerland as a cooperative response to credit scarcity. Issues electronic CHW (WIR franc), pegged 1:1 to CHF, accepted only inside the network. New CHW enters mainly when the bank itself extends collateralized credit to members. Subsequent trades clear among members. Price parity is required. Early demurrage was abandoned. Account balances earn no interest; loans can be cheaper. It is a closed-circuit, bank-mediated system rather than pure peer-to-peer mutual credit, and it has remained stable for nine decades as a small complement to the Swiss franc.

A replacement is not a tool

Max Tegmark: We're Building A Replacement Species · Dr Brian Keating

In this conversation, MIT physicist Max Tegmark says the question is not only whether a machine can do harm. It is whether people will still be in charge when it matters. He says today's systems are not yet trustworthy enough for real-world power. What is wanted is a controllable tool.

He says a company can grow so large and so hard to see into that it starts to act like a government, even a ministry of truth. His remedy is the same kind of standard a drug or a vehicle must meet before it is let out. Slowing a reckless release would not stop useful research. It would make the maker show that the system is safe. He also says the systems are already moving from copying people to finding things on their own.
