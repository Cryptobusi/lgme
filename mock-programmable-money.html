Source

Optimizing lgme.app via Programmable Money Insights. Created 28 October 2026. It reads Jack Chapple, The Crazy Plan To Reset The World’s Money In 2027, and asks what this desk should change.

The share’s frame: physical money fixed anonymity, indifference, permanence, offline use, and finality in the object. Software money turns each of those into a dial someone else can turn.

Six dials

  1. Memory and privacy. A coin does not remember who held it. Software money can. The share cites e-CNY yield for registered wallets, Aadhaar bound to UPI, and Europe’s digital identity wallet kept apart from the central bank.
  2. A clock. Gesell, the 1932 Wörgl scrip, and Fisher’s stamp scrip. The share cites Shenzhen envelopes that expired, and relief cards that died on a date.
  3. An opinion. Food stamps, regional vouchers, and cashless debit cards. Singapore’s purpose-bound money is supposed to drop the condition at settlement and become ordinary credit.
  4. The grid. A blackout stops a terminal. The share cites Sweden keeping a cash reserve and requiring cash for essentials, and offline digital-cash trials that still needed a clock and a battery.
  5. The pause button. Cash settles when it changes hands. A custodial rail can freeze a balance without a prior order. The share cites the 2022 Canadian account freezes.
  6. A machine that pays. Agents are already being handed a checkout. The share cites HTTP 402, Visa, Mastercard, Stripe, and a cap on what an agent may spend.

The share sets two desks against each other. One dial is top-down: expiry, surveillance, a remote kill, and a cloud that must stay up. The other is this book: bilateral intent, a visible tape, constitutional refusal, and a trade that can wait out a partition.

What the mock would change

  1. Purpose-bound credit that ends. A daycare hour or a tool loan can carry a time and a category. When both seats sign, those tags come off. The credit does not keep a permanent file of how it was spent.
  2. A trade that waits out the dark. If the network is down, two people can still sign a credit on the spot. The queue uses a count, not the phone’s clock. When the line returns, the batch is written. It is not a second mint.
  3. An agent that must show the line. A machine may ask to settle. The answer is a shown memo, not a card network and not a new identity gate. No agent mints.
  4. No office pause. A balance here is a named clearing between seats, not a pool an office can freeze. Keys stay with the person. Refusal stays the third seat.

Floor stays senior. Same rules bind state, firm, and person. An agent is a clerk. A condition that cannot be shown is not policy.
