How the order works

This is one cycle of the book, said in ordinary words. It is a rule for keeping accounts. It is not a machine this site is already running, and it is not a price. The live mission remains the Hederica charter. The tighter reading is the protocol page.

The problem

Two parties can make money and leave the risk with a third.

The state can print money, borrow, and tax. When that goes badly, the loss is spread across everyone who holds the currency.

A company can borrow and lend. The owners can keep the gains and leave a bad loan with someone else.

The person is the one left holding the bag. Prices rise, a bank is rescued, or a debt is quietly rewritten, and the person finds out afterward.

The order is a way to keep a second book. The same money can still exist. What changes is the order in which it is allowed to move, and the fact that every move has to be written down in public.

The three seats

A seat is a role.

The state may still print and tax. It may not hide the print.

The firm may still lend. A loan is a promise with a due date. It is not the same thing as cash in hand.

The person does not get a printing press. The person gets the right to read the public record and reject a line that skipped a step. That check does not depend on asking an office for permission.

One cycle, with numbers

Suppose 25,000 comes in. That is the inflow. The amount that must be set aside to live on, the floor, is 14,700.

The floor is paid first. It is not used as collateral, and it is not spent paying old debts. After the floor, 10,300 is left. That leftover is the only money later steps may touch.

If instead only 10,000 had come in, the floor would not be covered. The book would stop. No new money, no new loan, no leftover. The shortfall is written as a shortfall.

When the floor is covered, the steps run in this order.

  1. New money. New units can be created only from the leftover, and only up to a fixed share of it. If the leftover is 10,300 and the share is half, no more than 5,150 of new units may be created. A loan written in a side book does not count as new money.
  2. A loan. A loan has to say four things: who holds it, the amount printed on its face, how much less it is worth today, and the day it comes due. In the worked example the face is 10,000 and today’s discount is 6.5 percent. So today it is worth 10,000 × (1 − 0.065) = 9,350. Nobody is allowed to pretend the promise is worth the full 10,000 in cash.
  3. The public list. Every change is added to a list that already exists, and the new line is locked to the line before it. Change one old line and every later line no longer matches. If a debt is not on that list, it is not a debt of this book.
  4. Fees. The cost of running the book, and the cost of the risk being taken, come out before anyone is handed a profit. A fee cannot be waived after the fact to make the numbers look better.
  5. The clerk. A clerk then checks that the steps happened in this order. The clerk can move the floor payment, mark a skipped step, and throw out an old loan that someone tried to extend without writing a new one. The clerk cannot decide to print extra money, and cannot make an exception because someone asked nicely.
  6. The real market. The book shows how easy it actually is to buy and sell. If few people want the note, the book says so. It does not spend money to hold the price up and call that a stable market.
  7. What is left. Take the leftover after the floor. Subtract the fees and today’s value of the loan. What remains belongs to the person who did the work. If the subtraction comes out below zero, the book writes zero. It does not rename the loss as an asset or push it into next year.
  8. Paying debt down. That remainder is used to cancel debt. Extending an old loan is not a free continuation. It is a new loan, so it needs a new face amount, a new discount, and a new due date. Silence is not a way to leave a debt unpaid forever.

The floor is the first stage. New money is the second. The loan, the public list, the fees, the clerk, the real market, what is left, and paying debt down are the rest. Nine stages, in that order. Skip one and the line does not count.

What “on the tape” means

The tape is the public list. Each line contains the fingerprint of the line before it, plus this line’s facts: which step, which amounts, and whether the book halted or settled. Anyone can recompute the fingerprint. If it does not match, the line was altered.

A rule that never got written on the tape cannot bind the person. The person does not have to win an argument with an office. The person can see that the order was skipped and refuse the line.

How it would be taken up

It is meant to start small.

First, one household, farm, or company keeps this book beside its ordinary books. Living costs are written on the public list before any new credit is extended.

Then firms use it with each other. An invoice is not treated as cash. It clears at a discount that reflects how likely it is to be paid. A line that skips the floor is refused.

Only after that does the idea meet larger public books. If a cycle produces a leftover, that leftover retires debt. If a cycle produces no leftover, the book writes zero. It does not create fake reserves to make a deficit look balanced.

What this does not do

It does not move a wallet. It does not set a price. It does not replace the law or the charter.
