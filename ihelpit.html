Next implementation: making lgme easy for older adults

A PLOS ONE review notes that older adults, the main beneficiaries of time banks, face digital-literacy barriers, security anxiety, and hesitation to ask for help. Cryptographic timestamps solve only half of this. The screen has to keep protocol jargon out of the way.

Passkeys and in-person vouching

Proposed, not built. A fingerprint, a face unlock, or a 4-digit PIN would quietly create the device key in the background. A trusted neighbor could vouch in person with a 60-second QR handshake. No seed phrase. No social-media gate. No wallet on the first screen.

Plain-language labels

Proposed, not built. The rulebook can keep Sovereign Provenance terms. The screen would say Community Receipt Book instead of The Tape, Both People Confirmed instead of Bilateral Attestation, and Ask for Help, Log Help Given, Offer Goods, Community Goals, and Shared Upkeep instead of Want, Did, Sell, Wish, and Duty.

Voice, undo, and a caregiver

Proposed, not built. Large print, one question per screen, and voice-to-text. A 15-minute undo window before a Hedera note becomes final. An optional Caregiver Proxy mode. A one-tap printable monthly receipt summary.
