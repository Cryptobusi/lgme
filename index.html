Schelling self-management of profits

Thomas C. Schelling, “Egonomics, or the Art of Self-Management,” American Economic Review 68(2): 290–294 (1978).

The person is two selves in conflict. The present self prefers immediate extraction; the future self prefers durable rules. Preferences reverse, so pure intention fails. The remedy is precommitment: external barriers and bright-line rules the present self cannot casually reverse. Schelling’s opening example is the Christmas club account—the bank creates walls that protect the money from the depositor’s own later self.

In lgme the same logic binds profit. Floor Basket is paid first. Mint is public schedule only. Charge and risk are named before residual. Remainder (including zero) is titled. An unpublished change is not a change. These are Schelling barriers. The present self that would widen the spread or privatize the print must violate a visible categorical rule. The person stays the third seat because the protocol has already restrained the profit impulse.
