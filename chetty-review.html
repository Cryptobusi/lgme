Executive thesis

Harvard economist Raj Chetty’s empirical research proves that upward economic mobility in the United States has collapsed from 92% to a 50–50 coin flip, and that cross-class social capital (“economic connectedness”) is its single strongest predictor. However, Chetty’s policy prescription relies entirely on top-down institutional relocation and political will—mechanisms structurally blocked by entrenched incumbents. By operationalizing the Sovereign Provenance Protocol (Floor → Mint → Issue → Tape) and bilateral mutual-credit clearing (lgme.app), the Egonomist can mint verifiable social capital and local liquidity directly where people stand, without waiting for zoning boards or federal appropriations.

1. Empirical review: Raj Chetty’s diagnosis and prescription

In his WBUR Festival conversation with Michael Jonas, reported by CommonWealth Beacon (Jonas, 2026), Opportunity Insights director Raj Chetty synthesizes two decades of longitudinal tax and social network data into a stark portrait of American economic stagnation:

  • The collapse of absolute mobility. Among children born in 1940, 92% grew up to earn more than their parents (inflation-adjusted in their mid-30s). For the 1990 birth cohort, upward mobility fell to roughly 50%, turning the American Dream into a coin toss.
  • Hyperlocal geography over macro policy. Drawing on anonymized tax records covering 20 million children across 740 metropolitan and rural regions, Chetty demonstrates that life outcomes diverge sharply across adjacent neighborhoods and zip codes rather than broad state-level tax or regulatory regimes.
  • Cross-class social capital as the primary driver. Analyzing billions of social ties, Chetty identifies “economic connectedness”—friendships, mentorships, and civic interactions bridging low-income and higher-income residents—as the single strongest empirical predictor of upward mobility ever measured.
  • Top-down institutional remedies. Arguing that upward mobility generates positive fiscal externalities (higher GDP alongside reduced incarceration and healthcare expenditures), Chetty proposes expanding housing vouchers (building on HOPE VI), integrating low-income families into affluent neighborhoods, redesigning campuses and civic spaces to foster cross-class mixing, and mobilizing political coalitions to overcome zoning resistance.

Three foundational flaws in the relocation model

The proximity fallacy vs. sovereign standing

Chetty’s framing forces him to defend against the critique that low-income families merely need affluent neighbors to “sprinkle magic mobility dust” on them. Without a constitutional Third Seat, relocating a family into an affluent zip code without independent economic standing turns integration into dependency. Genuine agency requires equal standing alongside the State and the Firm, anchored by an irrevocable right of refusal (Force of the No).

The ledger blind spot

Opportunity Insights measures mobility exclusively through IRS tax-return income. As lgme.app demonstrates, conventional banking and tax ledgers register vital neighborhood production—eldercare, peer tutoring, tool libraries, local repair—as zero because verification and clearing fees exceed the transaction’s face value. Low-mobility (“red”) zip codes are not devoid of human productivity; they are starved of a local clearing instrument.

The political-will trap

Chetty acknowledges that policymakers already know which interventions work, yet affluent incumbents block housing integration. Relying on the beneficiaries of the existing asset ledger to vote away their zoning privileges is a structural dead end that perpetuates institutional gridlock.

3. The Egonomist mandate: can, should, and must

To translate Chetty’s macro-empirical validation of social capital into an actionable, sovereign economic architecture, the Egonomist framework establishes a three-tier operational mandate across capability, strategy, and constitutional safeguards.

What the Egonomist can do (immediate operational capability)

  • Operationalize “economic connectedness” via lgme.app. While commercial social media platforms algorithmically segregate users by socioeconomic status, lgme.app provides passkey- or authenticator-secured, both-sides-confirmed IdidIt (verified receipt) records for local work circles (Garden & Tool Library, Peer Tutoring, Local Service, Health) without crypto wallets or middleman fees. Campuses and neighborhoods can deploy cross-demographic clearing circles immediately.
  • Make invisible social capital self-sovereign. Whereas Opportunity Insights relies on proprietary corporate social network datasets to infer human ties from the outside, lgme.app enables individuals to own, verify, and export their own constitutive record of civic reliability and completed deeds.

What the Egonomist should do (strategic and narrative positioning)

  • Reframe mobility maps as ledger maps. In publications on egonomicanonymous.live, leverage Chetty’s 92%-to-50% collapse as the empirical entry point, demonstrating that red-versus-green opportunity maps actually chart where private credit flows versus where local reciprocity goes unrecorded.
  • Mint opportunity where people stand. Shift public discourse from uprooting families to chase scarce green zip codes toward deploying mutual credit (Issue / Named Bill) and senior subsistence (Floor) so any neighborhood becomes a high-mobility zone without displacement.
  • Architect cross-seat cohort bridges. Design lgme.app pilot onboarding explicitly around Chetty’s bridging mechanism—linking university students, retirees, gig workers, and local trades in shared clearing circles to accelerate intergenerational skill transfer and mentorship.

What the Egonomist must do (non-negotiable constitutional safeguards)

  • Enforce the constitutional sequence (Floor → Mint → Issue → Tape). Social capital mobilized without a senior subsistence Floor degrades into precarious gig-economy exploitation. The protocol must strictly prohibit platform rent extraction (rejecting the 10%+ take rates of incumbent apps) and opaque, unbacked credit issuance.
  • Preserve off-network privacy and irrevocable refusal. Unlike centralized big-data surveillance models, the Egonomist ledger must keep health and personal identifiers off-network, require explicit bilateral consent before any entry is recorded (“both people say yes”), and protect the Third Seat’s zero-cost right to decline any exchange.
  • Prevent speculative capture of the commons. Community and campus deeds must strictly generate non-speculative mutual credit (each verified task earns a small Creo (currently 0.01), spent once). Financializing social capital into a tradable external token would immediately re-enable extractive capture by outside capital. Deeds earn Creo, not coins; $lgme stays pegged to US$ and is never minted from deeds.
